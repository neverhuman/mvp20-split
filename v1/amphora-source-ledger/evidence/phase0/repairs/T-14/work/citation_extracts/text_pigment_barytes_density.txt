      <h1>Barytes</h1>

<h2>About Barytes</h2>

<p>Barytes, also known as barium sulfate (BaSO₄), is a naturally occurring inorganic mineral and the primary ore of barium. It is a white, opaque powder with high specific gravity (about 4.5 g/cm&sup3;). These key properties, especially density and chemical inertness, make barytes a preferred filler and extender pigment in industrial formulations.</p>

<p>Widely used in <a href="https://www.20microns.com/applications/paints-coatings">paints and coatings</a>, barytes reduces the need for expensive pigments like titanium dioxide and lowers material cost. Its Mohs hardness (3.0&ndash;3.5) lends durability and scratch resistance to end products.</p>

<h2>Key Benefits</h2>

<ul>
	<li><strong>High density:</strong>&nbsp;Specific gravity (~4.5 g/cm&sup3;) delivers strong filler power, adding substantial weight to formulations without compromising stability.</li>
	<li><strong>Low oil absorption:</strong>&nbsp;Needs less resin or binder in coatings&mdash;controls viscosity, maintains gloss, and helps reduce costs.</li>
	<li><strong>Gloss improvement:</strong>&nbsp;Fine grades enable smooth, high-gloss finishes and promote long-term gloss retention.</li>
	<li><strong>Chemical inertness:</strong>&nbsp;Resistant to acids, alkalis, and UV, supporting lasting durability in coatings and plastics.</li>
	<li><strong>Cost &amp; VOC reduction:&nbsp;</strong>Acts as an extender pigment, reducing dependence on titanium dioxide&mdash;cutting costs and lowering VOC emissions.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Available Grades</h2>

<ul>
	<li><strong>Baryte chips:</strong>&nbsp;Coarse natural lumps for heavy-fill, large-volume applications (e.g., drilling, radiation shielding).</li>
	<li><strong>Micron baryte:</strong>&nbsp;Finely ground powder for fillers in paints, plastics, and rubber.</li>
	<li><strong>Grey baryte:</strong>&nbsp;Natural, lower-whiteness grade for cost-sensitive, high-density formulations.</li>
	<li><strong>Barigloss:&nbsp;</strong>Ultra-pure, polished barytes for high-gloss coatings and specialty plastics.</li>
	<li><strong>Micro Basofix:</strong>&nbsp;Ultrafine, precipitated barytes for topcoats and cosmetic use.</li>
	<li><strong>Basofix:</strong>&nbsp;Technically milled barytes for demanding industrial extender and filler applications.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<p><strong>Q: What is Barytes (Barium Sulfate)?</strong></p>

<p>A: Barytes (BaSO₄) is a white, high-density, non-toxic mineral, valued as a durable filler and extender due to its chemical stability and moderate hardness.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What are the main uses of Barytes?</strong></p>

<p>A: It is used as a filler in paints, coatings, plastics, rubber, ceramics, and paper. The primary application is as a high-density weighting agent in oil and gas drilling fluids. It is also found in automotive components and medical imaging.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What grades of Barytes do you offer?</strong></p>

<p>A: We offer Baryte Chips, Micron Baryte, Grey Baryte, Barigloss, Micro Basofix, and Basofix&mdash;ranging from coarse chips to ultrafine precipitated powders for everything from drilling muds to premium coatings.</p>

<p><strong>Q: How does Barytes enhance paints and coatings?</strong></p>

<p>A: It increases film thickness, improves gloss and smoothness, strengthens durability, and partially replaces TiO₂ without sacrificing stability. It also boosts gloss retention and hardness.</p>

<p><strong>Q: Can barytes help reduce VOCs and costs?</strong></p>

<p>A: Yes. Its low oil absorption and ability to replace costly pigments allow higher filler loads, reducing binder and solvent requirements&mdash;resulting in lower VOCs and overall costs.</p>

        function setCookie(name, value, days) {
            const date = new Date();
            date.setTime(date.getTime() + (days * 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000));
            document.cookie = name + "=" + value + ";expires=" + date.toUTCString() + ";path=/";
        }

        // Get a cookie
        function getCookie(name) {
            const cname = name + "=";
            const decodedCookie = decodeURIComponent(document.cookie);
            const ca = decodedCookie.split(';');
            for (let i = 0; i < ca.length; i++) {
                let c = ca[i].trim();
                if (c.indexOf(cname) === 0) {
                    return c.substring(cname.length, c.length);
                }
            }
            return "";
        }
