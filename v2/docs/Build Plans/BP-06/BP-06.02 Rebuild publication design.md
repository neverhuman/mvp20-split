# BP-06.02 rebuild publication

- Design status: B2 physical implementation direction; no delivery credit from design.
- Owner: Knowledge replay, private rebuild persistence and authenticated rebuild transport.
- Parent: [canonical child](BP-06.02%20Typed%20graph%20authoring%20and%20replay.md).

An accepted graph revision already has immutable full snapshot content and an authoritative log/head. A rebuild must not invent another accepted change, alter a campaign pin or increment the semantic revision merely because storage was reconstructed. Distinguish the semantic head from its private materialized copy.

Rebuild from the accepted log under the stored graph profile, through an exact watermark H. A verified immutable replay cursor retains the complete state and seen change IDs, then consumes only ordered accepted tails through H2. It rejects gaps, duplicates, changed hashes, foreign heads and watermark regression. Canonical semantic bytes must remain identical to the accepted revision; timestamps/build IDs never enter the semantic digest. No LLM or current source payload consumption occurs during historical replay.

Add a forward migration after knowledge_0001 for durable scoped rebuild intent, immutable candidate materializations, immutable terminal results and a mutable publication pointer. A candidate binds its build identity, attempt, exact semantic head and canonical snapshot bytes. Publication takes the same short graph lock as ordinary acceptance, compares the full authoritative head and switches only the matching materialization pointer together with its terminal result. Log and semantic head remain authoritative. Historical immutable reads select only a published materialization for that exact semantic reference, with original B1 snapshots as the fallback.

An ordinary accepted edit can advance while rebuilding. A stale candidate cannot replace its newer semantic head. Bounded catch-up creates another immutable candidate; after the configured attempt budget, persist a deferred result and leave the previous complete snapshot readable. A new request can resume work later. An interrupted nonterminal request retries under its original identity and reconstructs from the accepted log; partial candidates never become visible. Competing identical requests reconcile the first terminal result. Changed actor/content under one rebuild ID conflicts.

The owner captures the initial exact head when first registering a rebuild ID; callers do not supply accepted records or a substitute snapshot. Use a separate immutable candidate ID for each local build attempt. Concurrent retries may prepare different private candidates, but only one terminal result/publication wins under the graph lock. The bounded catch-up budget applies per executing request; it is not a claim that all interrupted retries create at most that many audit rows. Avoid holding a database connection/session lock across CPU replay or external I/O. No cleanup may erase accepted history or a published materialization.

The authenticated rebuild boundary needs a distinct rebuild_graph grant, bounded request/status DTOs and exact actor binding. Proposal and rebuild statuses stay distinct. Rebuild observations are not scientific review or a renewed source-use license. Source rights are rechecked only when consuming payloads, not by reinterpreting old accepted metadata.

B2 verification covers fixed-watermark replay, incremental catch-up, same-head publication, stale CAS, exhausted contention budget, original-head readability on failure and exact retry. B3 adds actual process death and correction/publication race barriers plus QEV-02/03/04 and TRACE-04. V1 independently installs/replaces the owner and preserves public histories. No existing migration or accepted canonical bytes may be rewritten to introduce this projection boundary.
