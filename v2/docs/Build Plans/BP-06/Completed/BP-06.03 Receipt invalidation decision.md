## Concrete case

A campaign pins issuer Research, subject S, purpose P and receipt revision1. Research later learns new evidence and supersedes its accepted revision1 with revision2 while the campaign is still waiting. Should that campaign still be permitted to consume its original revision1 receipt, or should the correction prevent that pending consumption?

Current Runtime validates exactly the pinned issuer/scope/purpose/revision/subject and output type. It rejects a document whose outcome is superseded. It has no issuer-currentness/invalidation input at that boundary: presenting a separate superseded notice does not make the earlier accepted document invalid. The new pure disposition probe demonstrates that distinction without claiming an actual campaign commit. The existing PostgreSQL identity test also confirms that changing revision under the same receipt ID conflicts; revisions are not part of that immutable key.

B2 requires a new revision and invalidation notice, and preservation of old campaign history. Those requirements establish immutable history but do not state whether unconsumed receipts in an active campaign lose eligibility. The user's between-campaign human-edit rule does not settle autonomous evidence corrections. These alternatives change execution policy and integration scope, so this is an owner decision rather than a routine implementation detail.

## Option A - invalidate pending consumption (not selected)

A correction emits a new immutable receipt with a fresh ID and explicit predecessor linkage, plus an authenticated invalidation notice. Once Runtime durably accepts that notice, it must reject subsequent consumption of the old receipt, including a prepared but not yet committed acceptance. Accepted campaign history is never rewritten or automatically rerun. An affected open wait exposes a named blocked/replan outcome for autonomous handling; it does not silently change its expected revision or require routine human approval.

The correction and notice must remain durable in the issuing owner; delivery uses stable identities and reconciliation. Runtime stores notices under issuer authority and serializes invalidation versus acceptance at its own commit boundary. If acceptance commits first, preserve it; if invalidation commits first, prevent acceptance. This policy becomes effective when Runtime records the notice, not globally at the instant the issuer creates a correction. Stronger synchronous cross-owner revocation would require a separately scoped protocol.

B2 would implement owner correction chains/notice contracts and their durable/public boundary. B3 would add Runtime authenticated notice ingestion and acceptance rechecks, with race/recovery tests. This extends the existing Runtime wait behavior under the current BP-06 consumer integration scope; BP-04 remains closed. No backward schema or historical receipt rewrite is proposed.

## Option B - preserve the active campaign snapshot (selected)

Corrections affect future campaign selection. Running campaigns can consume their originally pinned accepted receipts even after an issuer creates a correction. Owner history exposes new revisions and notices, but no new Runtime invalidation command or active-wait revocation check is added. An explicit separately authorized cancellation remains possible. This keeps campaign reproducibility strongest but allows a pending step to use evidence already corrected by its issuer.

B2 would implement immutable owner correction/notice history and current-selection reads. B3 would prove new campaigns select revision2, old pins remain unchanged and superseded notice documents cannot themselves satisfy waits. This deliberately does not claim revocation of the original accepted document.
